# Further Reading

Explore these readings for interesting perspectives on AI, creativity, and scientific and medical visualization.

## Creativity and Visual Practice

| Name | Link | Summary |
|------|------|---------|
| In Defense of the Detour: On craft, A.I. and what gets lost when we skip the slow part. | [Read article](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/09/27/opinion/ai-art-creativity.html) | Designer Giorgia Lupi reflects on how the detours, exploration, and judgment involved in making creative work can be lost when AI delivers an output before the thinking begins. |
| What We Risk Losing When Creating Gets Easy: Friction, Judgment, and Critical Reflective Practice with Generative AI in Creative Work | [Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07506) | Shehryar Saharan, Shehroze Saharan, Roxanne Ziman, Nicholas Woolridge, and Gaël McGill argue that the friction of making helps develop creative judgment. This position paper proposes critical reflection on when to use—or refuse—generative AI. Accepted to the Reflection in Creative Experience workshop at Creativity & Cognition 2026. |
| “It Looks Sexy but It’s Wrong”: Tensions in Creativity and Accuracy Using GenAI for Biomedical Visualization | [Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14494) | Ziman, Saharan, McGill, and Garrison examine how generative AI can produce visually compelling biomedical imagery while introducing serious inaccuracies, highlighting the difference between aesthetic quality and scientific validity. |
| Integrating Artificial Intelligence Into Medical Illustration: A Balance of Promise and Precaution | [Read paper](https://doi.org/10.1097/SLA.0000000000007208) | Cohen, Baimas-George, Dunham, and Luks consider opportunities and risks of AI in medical illustration, including the balance between efficiency, visual communication, and professional responsibility. |

## Medical Illustration and Image Accuracy

| Name | Link | Summary |
|------|------|---------|
| Investigating the Use, Role, and Accuracy of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Anatomical Illustration | [Read paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/17453054.2026.2692359) | Erolin compares traditional anatomical illustrations, AI-generated images, and AI-edited illustrations, offering a nuanced look at AI as a possible enhancement to illustration workflows. |
| Bias, Representation, and Clinical Fidelity in AI-Generated Images for Medical Education: A Systematic Literature Review | [Read paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-02608-3) | Alon, Shoval, and Levkovich review 36 studies and identify representational bias and clinical fidelity as important concerns, including demographic skew, anatomical inaccuracies, and labeling errors. |
| Evaluation of Generative Artificial Intelligence Models in Producing Anatomically Accurate Illustrations: A Comparative Study of Text-to-Image Generators | [Read paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/ca.70189) | Strugała and colleagues compare six image generators using standardized anatomical illustration prompts. Their findings emphasize differences between tools, difficulties with complex structures, and the need for expert verification. |
